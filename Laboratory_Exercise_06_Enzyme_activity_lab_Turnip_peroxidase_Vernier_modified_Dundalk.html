7  Enzyme Activity: Turnip Peroxidase

7.1 Objectives

At the completion of this lab, students will be able to:

  1. Define the term enzyme and identify the class of macromolecules to which enzymes belong.

  2. Identify the substrate and products of the peroxidase-catalyzed reaction.

  3. Explain the role of guaiacol in this reaction.

  4. Use a colorimeter to detect product formation during an enzyme-mediated reaction.

  5. Determine the effects of the following conditions on the rate of an enzyme-catalyzed reaction:

    1. Substrate concentration

    2. Enzyme concentration

    3. Temperature

    4. PH

    5. The inhibitor hydroxylamine

  6. Use Excel, or a suitable software program, to plot the following:

    1. Raw data versus time
    2. Reaction rate versus treatment (substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, temperature, pH, and presence of inhibitors)

7.3 Introduction

As you have learned in the lecture part of this course, enzymes are protein molecules that catalyze various reactions in living organisms. They allow organisms to carry out complex chemical activities at relatively low temperatures and are highly specific for one or a group of related reactions. Enzymes bring substrates together in a manner that increases the likelihood that a reaction will occur. One of the unique features of enzyme-catalyzed reactions is that enzymes are not consumed during the process. That is, enzymes stay unchanged during the reaction and can be reused repeatedly.

Many factors can affect enzyme activity, and in this laboratory, we will study the effects of substrate concentration, enzyme concentration, temperature, pH, and inhibitors on enzyme activity (Pitkin, 1992).

In this series of experiments, you will use enzyme peroxidase isolated from turnips. Peroxidases catalyze the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide. In reactions catalyzed by peroxidase, reducing agents, such as guaiacol, donate electrons and hydrogen atoms, while hydrogen peroxide acts as the oxidizing agent. These reactions result in the formation of water and an oxidized organic compound, tetraguaiacol, as shown in the equation below:

4 H₂O₂ + 4 guaiacol → tetraguaiacol + 4 H₂O

Guaiacol acts as the hydrogen donor (i.e., it is the reducing agent). When guaiacol is oxidized, it changes from a colorless liquid to a dark orange-brown compound (tetraguaiacol). In this experiment, blue light (470 nm) will be passed through the solution, and a colorimeter will measure the amount of light transmitted. This measurement is based on the absorption of blue light by the brown-colored solution (Enzyme Activity Guided Inquiry Lab: Turnip Peroxidase, 2018).

Lab Activity 5.1: Practice experiments

  1. Obtain and wear gloves and goggles

  2. Practice run #1: 5-minute data collection

    1. Set the collection time. To set the collection time, you need to find the sampling time button, which looks like a clock on the upper right-hand side of the top bar. When you activate this program, you should set the collection duration for 300 seconds (5 min.) and collect each sample every 30 seconds (30 seconds/sample)

    2. Add the following to the cuvette.

      1. 2.2 ml of distilled water

      2. 400 µl of turnip extract

    3. Calibrate the sensor

      1. Press the (<or>) button to select the 470 nm wavelength. You will not have to do this during the other runs.

      2. Close the colorimeter lid. Press and hold the CAL button until the red light begins to flash.

        1. The absorbance should read 0.001 or less. If the display is set for transmittance, then the reading should be approximately 100%.

        2. When the light stops flashing, the system is calibrated.

    4. Test the sample

      1. Open the lid and add 400 µl of the substrate mix to the cuvette.

      2. Close the lid at once and hit the collect button (The green button at the right of the top bar)

      3. Record the data from your 5-minute experiment in table 5.6 of the Results Section.

  3. Practice run #2: 1 minute data collection

    1. Set the collection time. To set the collection time, you need to find the sampling time button that looks like a clock on the upper right-hand side of the top bar.       1. Add the following to the cuvette.

        1. 2.2 ml of distilled water

      1. Close the colorimeter lid. Press and hold the CAL button until the red light begins to flash.

  1. Interpret your data: How did reaction rate change with increasing substrate concentration? Explain biologically.

  2. Compare the effects of temperature and pH on enzyme activity. Which had the greatest impact and why?

    2. Did the result support or reject your hypotheses explain why?

  10. Research and choose an enzyme related to genetic disorder. Write a summary of the enzyme effects and their influences on the body.

  11. Imagine you are working in an international research laboratory as a prominent research scientist. Knowing that each enzyme has a set of optimal conditions (pH, temperature). Describe how you will go about to find out the best conditions for a newly discovered enzyme.

7.8 Appendix: Enzyme Activity and Experimental Methods Terms

Term Definition
Absorbance A measure of how much light is absorbed by a solution; used to estimate concentration of a product.
Active Site The region of an enzyme where the substrate binds and the reaction occurs.
Calibration The process of adjusting an instrument to ensure accurate measurements.
Colorimeter An instrument used to measure the absorbance of light by a solution.
Control A standard for comparison in an experiment where variables are kept constant.
Cuvette A small transparent container used to hold samples for spectrophotometer or colorimeter readings.
Data Collection The process of gathering measurements during an experiment.
Denaturation A change in protein structure that causes loss of function, often due to extreme temperature or pH.
Enzyme A protein that speeds up chemical reactions in living systems without being consumed.
Enzyme Concentration The amount of enzyme present, which can affect the rate of a reaction.
Experimental Variable A factor that is changed or tested in an experiment (e.g., pH, temperature).
Graphing The process of visually representing data to analyze trends.
Hydrogen Peroxide (H2O2) A chemical compound that serves as the substrate for peroxidase in this experiment.
Inhibitor A substance that decreases or stops enzyme activity.
Optimal Conditions The specific conditions (temperature, pH, concentration) at which an enzyme works best.
pH A measure of how acidic or basic a solution is.
Reaction Mixture The combined solution of enzyme, substrate, and other components used in an experiment.
Reaction Rate The speed at which reactants are converted into products.
Sampling Interval The time between each data collection point during an experiment.
Slope The rate of change in a graph, often used to determine reaction rate.
Spectrophotometer An instrument used to measure how much light a sample absorbs at a specific wavelength.
Substrate The molecule upon which an enzyme acts.
Substrate Concentration The amount of substrate available in a reaction, affecting reaction rate.
Temperature A measure of heat that can influence enzyme activity and reaction rates.
Transmittance The amount of light that passes through a sample.
Turnip Peroxidase An enzyme extracted from turnips that catalyzes the breakdown of hydrogen peroxide.
Wavelength The distance between peaks of light waves, measured in nanometers, used in absorbance readings.
